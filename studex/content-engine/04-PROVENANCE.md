# Provenance, watermarking and the commodity problem

**Prepared:** 25 Aug 2026

Two things changed in August 2026 that a content business has to design around.
Both are recent enough that most competitors have not adjusted, which is an
opportunity if we handle it deliberately.

---

## 1. Claude text is watermarked, globally, since 2 August 2026

### The facts

Anthropic embeds a statistical watermark in text generated by Claude models
launched on or after **2 August 2026**. It uses a variant of the SynthID-Text
method: the sampling process is nudged toward particular word choices, creating a
pattern a detector holding Anthropic's key can score.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Scope | Platform API, claude.ai, Claude Code, and Claude via AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft Foundry |
| Reach | Applied **worldwide**, not only in the EU — no durable way to scope by region |
| Reason | EU AI Act Article 50(2); Anthropic signed the Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content in July 2026, alongside roughly 190 signatories |
| Older models | Covered by an EU transition period; watermarking being added over the coming months |
| Images | Not a pixel watermark — cryptographically signed **C2PA** provenance metadata on generated PNG, JPG and SVG |
| Detection | Anthropic is building a public detection API; details not finalised |
| Other labs | OpenAI, Google, Meta and Microsoft signed the same Code and are implementing their own marks |

### The limits, stated by Anthropic

- It answers "how likely is it that Claude was involved", not "who wrote this".
- It cannot distinguish "Claude wrote this" from "Claude heavily edited this".
- **Unreliable on short samples** — fewer word choices, less signal. Confidence
  rises with length.
- Weak on fact-dense passages and code, where alternative phrasings are limited.
- Text where a human chose every word carries no mark.
- **Heavy rewriting can strip it.**
- False negatives are possible; human text summarised or combined with generated
  text may carry a signal.

### What this means for us

**Three practical consequences.**

**Disclose it.** If StudEx produces content for clients, the client must know the
output is detectably AI-involved. Not disclosing is a misrepresentation that
becomes checkable the moment the detection API ships. Put it in the service
agreement, not in a footnote.

**Long-form carries more signal than social.** A 1,500-word pillar is far more
detectable than a 30-word caption. Since the pillar is exactly where we want
heavy human editing anyway, the incentive points the right way.

**The human editing pass is now doing two jobs.** It was a quality gate. It is
now also provenance: a piece a person has genuinely rewritten is both better and
differently marked. That is a reason to strengthen gate 2, not to game it.

### Our position, stated plainly

> We use AI in content production. We disclose it. Every piece is built on
> research we can show you and edited by a person who is accountable for it. We
> do not attempt to defeat detection, and we will not tell you a machine did not
> help.

The one thing not to do is edit specifically to evade the watermark while telling
clients we disclose AI use. That is a small dishonesty that would undermine the
audit-trail argument the entire managed-agents offering is built on. If we are
selling provable accountability, we cannot be quietly gaming provenance.

### Disclosure standard

| Content type | Disclosure |
|---|---|
| Client-facing content we produce | Stated in the service agreement, and in the client's own record |
| StudEx published content | A standing note on the site about how we produce content |
| Investor material | Facts stand or fall on sources; disclosure in the data room |
| Client's customer-facing agent replies | The agent identifies as AI — already required by the launch pack |
| Generated images | C2PA metadata retained. **Do not strip it.** |

That last row matters and is easy to get wrong: image pipelines routinely strip
metadata on resize or re-export. Check that ours does not.

## 2. Commodity content is now worse than nothing

Google is aggressively noindexing commodity content, and the trade press is
running the "AI slop" argument in the same week it published the workflow method
we built from. The direction is unambiguous.

**The conclusion for the engine:** the pipeline has no value of its own. All of
the value is in the inputs. A well-built pipeline fed generic inputs produces
volume that will not be indexed, will not be cited, and trains an audience to
skip us.

This is why [02-CONSTANTS-AND-GAPS.md](02-CONSTANTS-AND-GAPS.md) audits the
inputs before any agent is built, and why every brief must name its non-commodity
element before it is accepted.

### The three defences

| Defence | Why it holds |
|---|---|
| **First-party data** | Cannot be generated. THE AUDIT results, our own operating numbers, client outcomes with permission. |
| **A named opinion with reasoning** | A position a competitor would dislike, argued. Models converge on the agreeable middle; disagreement is a human signal. |
| **Specificity that costs something** | Real prices, real dates, real failures. "We built a campaign in June and never shipped it, here is why" cannot be synthesised. |

All three are cheap for StudEx and expensive for a competitor with a content
tool. That is the whole strategy.

### The strongest asset available, and it is unbuilt

THE AUDIT — publishing our own score on our own ten-question test, **with the
failures visible** — satisfies all three defences at once. It is first-party, it
is an opinion competitors will dislike, and admitting failure is the most
specific thing a company can do.

It has been specified since 22 August, on an unmerged pull request, and nothing
has been built. It remains the highest-value content asset StudEx has, and it
costs nothing but the willingness to publish a bad score.

## What to add to the pipeline

Two additions to the checks in [01-DEFINITION-OF-DONE.md](01-DEFINITION-OF-DONE.md):

| Check | Where | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| Non-commodity element named on the brief | Orchestrator, at intake | Present and specific |
| Non-commodity element present in the finished piece | Score gate | Identified by section |
| AI involvement disclosed per the standard above | Score gate | Correct for the content type |
| C2PA metadata retained on any generated image | Score gate | Not stripped |

And one rule for the AI editor, already in its document: remove tells because
generated prose is bad prose, never to defeat detection.
