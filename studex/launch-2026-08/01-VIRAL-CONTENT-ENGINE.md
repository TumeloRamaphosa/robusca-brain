## The strategy in one paragraph

We are not going to make content about Studex. Content about your own company does not
travel. We are going to run a public argument that the AI agent industry is mostly faking
it, publish the test that proves it, take the test ourselves in public including where we
fail — and let the product be the thing people find at the end of the argument. The
argument is the content. The product is the conclusion.

This works right now for a specific and temporary reason: contrarian claims are the highest-
reach hook archetype, hard proof is the highest-saved, and the category's mood is scepticism.
Reach, trust and sales pitch point the same direction for once.

## The format rules (non-negotiable)

From the August 2026 sweep. These are constraints, not suggestions — every one is a
measured effect, and violating them wastes the angle.

| Rule | Number behind it |
|---|---|
| Hook lands in **1–3 seconds**. Reels ~1s, TikTok ~1.5s, Shorts ~2s. | The retention threshold moved from 5s (2024–25) to 3s |
| **No logo, no intro, no greeting** in the opening second | Most common cause of a good piece flopping |
| **Hot Take** or **Investigator** hooks for reach | ~140K avg views vs ~7.1K for story hooks — a 20x gap |
| **Proof Drop** hooks when the goal is saves | ~1,761 avg saves, highest in a 3,997-video dataset |
| **Greenscreen / screen-recording**, not talking head | 150K+ vs 56K avg views (~2.6x). Montage is worst at 12K |
| **Go long — 90 seconds plus** | 90s+ averages ~170K vs ~5K for sub-15s (31x). Ranking favours total watch time |
| **Prove the hook's claim within 10 seconds** | The lock-in zone. Unsubstantiated hooks read as bait and the curve collapses |
| **Direct address, eye contact**, name their situation in five words | Described as the quietly highest-performing pattern of 2026 |
| **Never open with "So the other day…"** | The 7.1K number above |
| **Measure 3-second retention, not views** | Views are a lagging output of the hook |

Never cross-post an identical cut. Same script, re-cut opening second per platform.

## Five series

One flagship, three engines, one authority play. Each has a distinct job. Do not blur them.

### Series A — **THE AUDIT** (flagship)
**Job:** define the exam. **Hook type:** Hot Take + Proof Drop. **Platform:** all + LinkedIn/YouTube long.

A public ten-question test for whether an AI vendor's "agent" is real, drawn directly from
what enterprise buyers and analysts now screen for. Published free, as a scored PDF and a
web page. No gate on the first version — gate the *benchmark results*, not the test.

Then we take it ourselves, on camera, and publish the score with the failures visible.

This is the campaign's spine because it does four jobs in one asset: it is a lead magnet, it
is inherently shareable, it sets evaluation criteria on ground where we win, and taking it
publicly with failures shown buys more credibility than any claim we could make. Posting the
flat graph outperforms posting the spike — and every competitor is incentivised to hide.

**The ten questions** (each one a standalone piece of content):
1. Show me the decision trace. Which tools were called, what came back, why each next step?
2. What does it remember from six months ago, and can it cite the source document?
3. When a tool call fails, does it retry and re-plan, or does it hand the user an error?
4. Is it monitored in production, or only in the demo environment?
5. Can a non-engineer change a governance policy, or does that need a ticket?
6. Is pricing per seat, or tied to outcomes?
7. What can it do without asking permission, and who decided that?
8. Where does our data physically live, and who else's data lives there?
9. How does it verify an action actually happened?
10. Show me one customer number you didn't choose.

### Series B — **THE GHOST TEST** (product engine)
**Job:** demonstrate the product. **Hook type:** Investigator. **Format:** screen recording.

Ask a question only a company's own history could answer. Show a generic assistant fail.
Show the Business Ghost answer it and cite the source. Under two minutes, no narration
needed beyond the setup.

This is the single most convertible format available to us, because it is a demo disguised as
a curiosity. Demonstrate; never announce. "We built X" is an announcement. X doing something
surprising is content.

Question bank: *Why did we drop that supplier in March? · What did we promise this client in
the second meeting? · Who has asked about this product and never bought? · What did we decide
about pricing and then quietly stop doing? · What's the thing we've now discussed four times
without deciding?*

That last one is the killer. Every company has one.

### Series C — **MIDNIGHT** (community engine)
**Job:** manufacture recurring content and community in one motion. **Format:** live → clips.

The Midnight Founders Club Friday pitch sessions, run live, cut into 90s+ clips all week. A
founder pitches; the Ghost is pointed at their business live; the agents produce something
useful in front of the audience.

A weekly ritual is the cheapest content engine that exists — it generates its own supply,
gives the audience a reason to return on a schedule, and the pitching founders bring their
own networks. It also feeds the escalator directly: tonight's pitch is next quarter's Rise
cohort member.

### Series D — **THE LEDGER** (trust engine)
**Job:** be the receipts. **Hook type:** Proof Drop. **Cadence:** monthly, fixed date.

Published numbers. Companies onboarded, agents deployed, tasks completed, tasks failed,
revenue, churn. Including the bad months. Especially the bad months.

Radical numeric transparency is close to free for us and expensive for anyone agent-washing.
It is also the only durable defence against being lumped in with them. Fixed date each month
— a ledger that skips a month tells its own story.

### Series E — **THE CORRIDOR** (authority play)
**Job:** third-party credibility. **Platform:** LinkedIn, YouTube long, earned press.

The SA–Russia trade corridor, the real partner companies, what actually moves between the
two markets. Long-form and pitched to journalists rather than posted.

Founder social is *push* — self-reported, and we control it, which is exactly why it carries
less weight. Independent coverage is *pull*: uncontrollable, therefore heavier. B2B needs
both and they are not substitutes. Series A–D build the audience; Series E converts audience
into institutional credibility. This is also the series that matters for governments and
enterprise procurement, who will not be moved by a Reel.

## Three scripts, production-ready

### Script 1 — "Only 130 are real" (Series A, flagship launch piece)
**Format:** greenscreen over the Gartner Hype Cycle chart. **Length:** ~100s. **Hook:** Hot Take.

```
[0:00 — no logo, no intro. Face already mid-sentence. On-screen text: "130"]

Gartner says there are about a hundred and thirty real AI agent companies in the world.
There are thousands claiming it.

[0:05 — LOCK-IN ZONE. Cut to the Hype Cycle chart, highlight "agent-washing"]

That's not me being cynical. In April, Gartner put out its first Hype Cycle just for
agentic AI, and it did something it has never done before — it named a specific scam in
the body of the report. Agent-washing. Taking a chatbot, or a scripted workflow, or an RPA
bot, calling it an autonomous agent, and charging enterprise money for it.

[0:22 — greenscreen: the MIT 95% stat]

Here's why it matters. An MIT study found ninety-five percent of enterprise AI pilots
produced no measurable impact on the P&L. Gartner reckons over forty percent of agentic
projects get cancelled by 2027. Companies aren't imagining it. They bought agents and got
macros.

[0:40 — direct address, eye contact]

So if you're about to buy one, here are the three questions that separate the real from
the repainted.

One. Show me the decision trace. Not a chat log — which tools it called, what came back,
why it chose the next step, and how it verified the thing actually happened. If they can't
show you that, there's nothing underneath.

[0:55]

Two. What does it remember? Persistent memory is on Gartner's own list of what makes an
agent an agent. Ask it something from six months ago. Ask it to cite the source. Most of
them can't, because there is no memory — there's a context window and a good demo.

[1:10]

Three. What happens when a tool call fails? A real agent retries and re-plans. A fake one
hands your staff an error message and becomes their problem.

[1:22 — close, one CTA]

I wrote the full ten-question version down, because we had to pass it ourselves before we
could sell anything. It's free, link's in the profile — and our own scorecard is in there
too, including the two we failed.
```

Note the close: giving away the payoff completely, and volunteering the failures. That is
what makes it travel rather than read as an ad.

### Script 2 — "The thing you've discussed four times" (Series B)
**Format:** screen recording, two windows. **Length:** ~90s. **Hook:** Investigator.

```
[0:00 — screen already recording, cursor in a chat box, no intro]

Every company has one decision it's discussed four separate times and never actually made.
I'm going to find ours.

[0:06 — LOCK-IN: type into a generic assistant, on screen]

First, a normal AI assistant. Watch.
> "What decision have we discussed repeatedly this year without resolving?"

[0:14 — it answers generically]

Right. It's guessing. It has to — it's never seen a single one of our meetings. It doesn't
know we exist.

[0:22 — switch window to the Business Ghost]

Now the same question, to a system that's been holding our company's memory since February.

[0:30 — the answer appears. Let it sit. No voiceover.]

[0:38]

Three mentions. Two meetings and a WhatsApp thread. It cited the dates. And look at the
last line — it noticed we stopped bringing it up right after the person who cared about it
left.

[0:50 — direct address]

That's the difference between a chatbot and a memory. The chatbot is smart about the world.
This one is smart about us. It doesn't get smarter because the model improved. It gets
smarter because it was here.

[1:05]

We call it a Business Ghost. One per company. It holds goals, meetings, customers,
projects, documents, decisions — and it doesn't resign.

[1:15 — CTA]

Ask your own company that question this week. Whatever comes back is either your memory
working, or the reason you need one.
```

### Script 3 — "The escalator" (Series E / positioning anchor)
**Format:** talking head with animated build of the four tiers. **Length:** ~2min. **Hook:** Hot Take.

```
[0:00]

Every AI company sells software to one type of customer. We built an escalator instead.

[0:07 — LOCK-IN: the four tiers build on screen]

Four tiers. At the top, the biggest companies in a country — twenty to a hundred of them.
Below that, hundreds of startups. Below that, the developers, the AI engineers, the
creative studios. And at the bottom, students, gamers, coders — people who haven't started
anything yet.

[0:25]

Everyone runs on the same infrastructure. Same memory layer, same agent operating system,
same cloud. And that one fact changes what's possible.

[0:35]

Because now work can flow down. A top-100 company has a project. It doesn't need to run a
procurement cycle to find a vendor — the startups two tiers down are already inside the
system, already know how to work in it, already have the agents running.

[0:50]

And talent can flow up. The student in our Arcade programme builds something on a Friday
night. That gets seen. She joins a startup in the Rise cohort. That startup delivers for an
enterprise. Three years later she's running the delivery.

[1:05]

That's not a funnel. A funnel loses people. An escalator carries them.

[1:12 — the honest part]

Now, the reason most people don't build this: it's four businesses, and each one is hard.
Talent programmes don't make money. Startup cohorts are slow. Enterprise sales take
eighteen months. Marketplaces are a cold-start nightmare.

[1:25]

The only way it works is if one thing serves all four. For us that's the memory layer.
Every company on every tier needs a permanent memory of itself, and once they have one
here, the rest of the escalator is right there.

[1:40 — close]

We're building it out of South Africa, for markets that never got the infrastructure the
first time round. Africa has fifty-four countries. Each one needs its own escalator.

That's the whole plan. Memory, workforce, market — in that order.
```

## Distribution

**Founder account originates. Brand accounts amplify.**
The @ramaphosatumelo account (86,846 followers per `STUDEX_OS.md`, needs re-baselining) is
the largest owned audience in the group, and founder-led origination outperforms brand-
account origination for B2B. Post from the founder first; brand accounts repost with a
different opening second.

**Platform jobs — do not treat them as one channel:**
- **LinkedIn** — where the buyer is. Series A, D, E. Text-first with the video as support.
  LinkedIn Lives are disproportionately valuable and underused; cap at two a month.
- **Instagram / TikTok** — reach and talent recruitment. Series B, C. This is where Arcade
  and Midnight get their supply.
- **YouTube** — the long-form archive. Full Midnight sessions, the escalator piece, full
  demos. This is the asset that still works in a year.
- **Earned press** — Series E only. Pitched, not posted.

**Paid:** none for the first 30 days. Let organic 3-second retention identify the winners,
then amplify only pieces that already cleared the bar organically. Boosting a piece with
weak retention buys reach for something that does not convert.

## Measurement

Log every piece in `content/performance-log.md`:

| Date | Piece | Series | Hook archetype | Format | Length | 3s retention | Saves | Shares | Profile visits | Signups |

**Reading it honestly:**
- **3-second retention is the primary metric.** Views are downstream of it.
- Minimum five pieces per hook archetype before concluding anything. One hit is noise.
- A sustained 10–15% retention lift across five pieces is real signal. A single spike is not.
- Watch the weekly retention trend, not individual posts. A curve creeping up over four
  weeks beats any one viral outlier.
- Kill any archetype that underperforms across five pieces, regardless of whose idea it was.

**The 30-day success condition** is not a view count. It is: *the Audit is being cited by
people who don't work here.* If procurement teams, consultants or competitors start
referencing the ten questions, the positioning has landed and everything else follows. If we
get ten million views and nobody repeats the argument, we made entertainment.
