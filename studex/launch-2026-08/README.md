## The short version

**How to describe it.** Three nouns, in order: **Memory → Workforce → Market**. Every box in
the architecture diagram is one of those three, and each one requires the one before it. The
one-liner: *"We give companies a memory that doesn't leave."* The structural version: Studex
is an escalator — students at the bottom, country-leading companies at the top, same
infrastructure carrying everyone, so work flows down it and talent flows up.

**The wedge.** In April 2026 Gartner named `agent-washing` an explicit procurement hazard and
estimated only ~130 of thousands of vendors are genuinely agentic. MIT found ~95% of
enterprise AI pilots produced no measurable P&L impact. The category is in a credibility
recession, and "our agents are powerful" is now a low-trust sentence. But the buyer's
checklist for a *real* agent — persistent memory, decision trace, permissions, isolation,
outcome pricing — is a list of things Studex already has and describes badly. So the
positioning is not "our agents are better." It is: **most AI agents are fake, here's the
test, here's us taking it.**

**The content.** Don't make content about Studex; run a public argument and let the product be
the conclusion. Flagship asset is a free ten-question audit anyone can run on any AI vendor —
including us, scored publicly, failures shown. Formats are dictated by the research: hook in
1–3 seconds, hot-take and investigator openers, greenscreen over talking head, 90 seconds and
up, and measure 3-second retention rather than views.

**The launch.** Business Ghost. One product, one price, one promise, one demo. Not the
ecosystem — a launch is an act of concentration, and eight simultaneous launches is zero.
Ghost is the memory layer, it demos in thirty seconds, it needs no marketplace liquidity, and
it makes Super Agents the obvious next purchase. New tier at R950 per company per month,
unlimited seats. Launch mechanic is Ghost Day: 20–30 companies onboarded live, week-one
results published.

**The blocker.** We have no instrumented outcome numbers for any of the four enterprise
deployments. A campaign premised on "everyone else overclaims" cannot itself overclaim. Fix
that first — and rotate the API keys flagged in June before pointing the internet at our
infrastructure.

**The thing to stop.** Take the token rail out of every deck and script until a lawyer has
given a written position. In South Africa that flow plausibly needs FSCA licensing, FIC
registration *and* a separate FinSurv authorisation for the cross-border leg, and utility
tokens are not carved out. Independently of the legal question, showing an enterprise
procurement team an unlicensed token while pitching them on governance undoes the entire
argument.
