## The insight worth having first

There was a tension I expected to have to manage, and it turns out not to exist.

The launch strategy positions Studex against **agent-washing** — receipts, audit trails,
publishing the flat graph rather than the spike. Luxury positioning normally pulls the
other way: aspiration, mystique, style over substance. Those look incompatible.

They converge on one word: **provenance.**

> **Ten years old in a category where the median vendor is sixteen months old.**

Runable was incorporated in April 2025. Grok Bot launched in August 2026. Gartner reckons
only about 130 of thousands of vendors claiming agentic capability are real. In that
market, a decade is simultaneously the luxury argument *and* the anti-hype argument. It is
the same fact doing both jobs.

That is why the anniversary is not a nice-to-have campaign. **It is the single most
valuable and least replicable asset the group owns**, and no competitor can buy it,
raise it, or ship it.

Hence the line the site is built on:

> ## A decade in the market. Not a moment in the hype cycle.

## Six mechanics of a high-end service

Luxury is not an aesthetic. It is a set of commercial behaviours, and the aesthetic
follows from them. These are ranked by how much they change, in order.

### 1. Selection, not sale

Luxury services do not sell. They **accept**.

Change the funnel language and the whole perception moves:

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| Sign up | Request an introduction |
| Book a demo | A conversation with the founder |
| Free trial | Assessment |
| Pricing page | By enquiry |
| Customer | Cohort member |
| Onboarding | Induction |

The ten-minute founder call already exists in the delivery model. Reframing it as an
**interview in both directions** costs nothing and is the highest-leverage change on this
list. "We are deciding as much as you are" makes the buyer want to qualify. And it has the
useful side effect of filtering out the clients who would destroy the margin.

### 2. Scarcity that is actually true

**Twelve companies per country, per year.** Cohorts close when full.

This has to be real, and here it is: the unit economics put steady-state delivery at
roughly four human hours per client per month, with break-even around twenty-one clients.
Capacity is genuinely constrained. Manufactured scarcity gets discovered and costs more
than it earned; constrained capacity honestly stated is unassailable — and it converts,
because a closing cohort is the only deadline a buyer believes.

### 3. Price as a signal, never a justification

Tom Ford does not publish thread count. A price defended by a feature list is a price
being negotiated.

- **Publish** Ghost (R950) and Standard (R2,500). These are self-serve; transparency helps conversion.
- **Do not publish** Company Builder upward. "By enquiry" from R5,000.
- **Never** a competitor comparison table. It concedes that we are in the same category as
  a $20 tool, and on a feature grid against Runable or MuleRun we lose — they have more
  compute at lower prices. The moment we compare, we have lost the frame.

### 4. Provenance, stated plainly and never explained

"Est. MMXVI." "Since 2016." Roman numerals on the anniversary.

State it once, at scale, and move on. The instinct to justify — *and here's why ten years
matters* — is the thing that undoes it. Let the buyer draw the conclusion; they will draw
it more forcefully than we could state it.

### 5. Restraint as evidence of confidence

The site says very little on purpose. A company explaining itself at length is negotiating.

This is also where luxury and the product design language agree, which resolves the
tension I opened with. Tom Ford's actual discipline is **reduction and precision** — black,
ivory, one gold detail, immaculate type, enormous space. That is the same discipline as
"the interface is a document, not a dashboard." Two registers, one grammar: the marketing
site is cinematic and spare, the product is quiet and functional. Both are reductive.

Luxury houses do exactly this — the campaign is theatrical, the boutique interior is calm.
What is *not* compatible is luxury-as-ornament: gold gradients, marble textures, swirling
filigree, Renaissance pastiche. That reads as costume.

### 6. The client is the hero

Tom Ford sells who you become, not what he made. We sell **what the company becomes** —
a business with a memory, a workforce, and access it did not have. Not "our platform has
persistent memory."

## The imagery direction, and the image you sent

The Michelangelo composite — *Creation of Adam*, Gucci-monogram shorts, our mark as the
divine spark — is a great internal piece and it should not be the brand. Four reasons,
and the fourth is the one that matters most.

**It is built on another house's trademark.** Gucci's monogram is registered. Putting it in
our brand imagery is a real exposure, and strategically it says the quiet part out loud:
*we borrow status.* A luxury house never wears someone else's monogram.

**Renaissance pastiche is the most-used cliché in crypto and AI marketing.** It is the
visual signature of precisely the hype cycle our headline distances us from. An
institutional buyer in Frankfurt or Kigali reads it as 2021, not 2026.

**The metaphor is grandiose in the exact month grandiosity stopped working.** "We are the
divine spark of AI" is the visual equivalent of "14 years of AI" — an unearned claim, and
we already decided to drop those.

**And it is the opposite of Tom Ford.** Tom Ford is reduction and absolute self-assurance.
Appropriating Michelangelo and Gucci is neither. Borrowed iconography is what a brand does
when it does not trust its own.

**But the instinct is right,** and worth taking seriously: you want the brand to feel
monumental and consequential. The way to get monumental is scale, silence and precision —
one image, enormous, perfectly shot, almost nothing else on the page.

**And there is a far better version of the same idea that you already own.** The Ankole
bull is *in the logo*. Ankole cattle are a genuine African symbol of wealth, lineage and
standing — and Studex Meat trades in Ankole beef. That is your own mythology, nobody else
owns it, and it ties the group's businesses together. Michelangelo belongs to everyone,
which is another way of saying it belongs to no one.

**The photographic direction I would brief:**

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Ankole horns, close, single light, near-black | Renaissance paintings, any era |
| Real African landscape at dusk, wide, empty | Stock "global business" imagery |
| Hands, documents, a signature — trade as craft | Robots, brains, neural-network graphics |
| Product and packaging shot like still life | Other brands' logos or monograms |
| One image per view, at scale | Collages and grids |
| Grain and shadow. Film, not render | Gradients, glows, purple-blue AI palette |

Where the composite *can* live: your personal social account, a founder's post, a group
chat. Clearly a joke, clearly yours, clearly not the brand.

## What the site does

Built at [`index.html`](index.html). Obsidian, ivory, one gold. Cormorant Garamond at
scale, Inter for body, and a great deal of empty space.

| Section | The job it does |
|---|---|
| Hero — mark, `MMXVI · MMXXVI`, the headline | Provenance before proposition. The date is the argument |
| The Decade | *"Most of this industry is younger than our filing cabinet."* Four dated marks, no adjectives |
| What We Do | Memory → Workforce → Market, numbered I/II/III. Three nouns, in order |
| Selection | *"We do not sell software. We accept companies."* Then the four terms — capacity, the conversation, your own environment, a written record |
| Enquire | "The tenth cohort opens this year." Outlined button, not filled. No form on the page |

Two deliberate choices worth defending:

**Roman numerals rather than "10 YEARS!"** An exclamation mark is a company celebrating
itself. `MMXVI · MMXXVI` is a company stating a fact and trusting you to read it.

**"Request an Introduction," outlined, not filled.** A solid button asks. An outlined one
offers. The difference is small and it is the whole posture.

Note also that the fourth Selection term — *"a written record… including the failures"* —
is the anti-agent-washing position translated into luxury register. Same argument, black
tie.

## Before this goes live

**Claims to verify.** Everything on the page is either supportable or needs checking:

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Registered 2016 | **Confirm.** Notebooks say "a decade of registration" |
| 2019 — first cross-border corridors | **Confirm** the year |
| 2023 — the Ghost | **Confirm** the year |
| "Four enterprise deployments live" | Supportable. **Do not upgrade to results** without instrumented numbers |
| Johannesburg · Kigali · Moscow · Dubai | **Confirm** we can substantiate a presence in each |

The whole campaign runs on not overclaiming. A page premised on a decade of provenance
cannot have a date on it that someone can disprove.

**Build next, in order:**

1. **An application form, not a signup.** Company, sector, markets, turnover band, and one
   free-text question: *what didn't get done last month that would have made money?* That
   answer is the qualification and the first Ghost entry.
2. **Replace the hero mark with one photograph** — Ankole horns, single light, near-black.
   The mark then moves to the masthead and footer only. This is the single biggest upgrade
   available and it needs a photographer, not a designer.
3. **Real numbers in The Decade**, once the pilot produces them. Corridors opened, cohort
   size, containers moved.
4. **A private cohort page** behind the application. Scarcity is more persuasive when part
   of the estate is visibly not for you.

**Leave off entirely:** the token and stablecoin, "14 years of AI", named client results,
any feature comparison, and any pricing above Standard.
