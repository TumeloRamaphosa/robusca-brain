## The Positioning Insight

I expected a tension between the anti-agent-washing launch strategy (receipts, publish
the flat graph) and luxury positioning (aspiration, mystique). **There isn't one — they
converge on provenance.**

> **Ten years old in a category where the median vendor is sixteen months old.**

Runable incorporated April 2025. Grok Bot launched August 2026. Gartner counts ~130 real
agent vendors out of thousands. A decade is simultaneously the luxury argument *and* the
anti-hype argument — the same fact doing both jobs. That makes the anniversary the single
most valuable and least replicable asset the group owns.

Headline: **"A decade in the market. Not a moment in the hype cycle."**

## Six Mechanics of a High-End Service

Ranked by impact. Luxury is commercial behaviour, not aesthetics.

1. **Selection, not sale.** "Request an introduction," not sign up. The ten-minute founder
   call becomes an **interview in both directions** — costs nothing, highest leverage on
   the list, and filters out the margin-destroying clients.
2. **Scarcity that is actually true.** Twelve per country per year — defensible because
   delivery genuinely runs ~4 human hours/client/month with break-even ~21 clients.
3. **Price as signal.** Publish Ghost (R950) and Standard (R2,500) — self-serve, transparency
   helps. "By enquiry" above. **Never a comparison table** — on a feature grid against
   Runable or MuleRun we lose, and the moment we compare we've lost the frame.
4. **Provenance stated once, never explained.** `Est. MMXVI`. The urge to justify undoes it.
5. **Restraint as confidence.** A company explaining itself at length is negotiating.
6. **The client is the hero.** Sell what the company becomes.

## Resolving the Design Tension

Tom Ford's actual discipline is **reduction and precision** — black, ivory, one gold,
immaculate type, enormous space. That is the *same grammar* as the product's "interface is
a document, not a dashboard." Two registers, one discipline: marketing site cinematic and
spare, product quiet and functional. Both reductive.

What is incompatible is luxury-as-**ornament** — gold gradients, marble, filigree,
Renaissance pastiche. That reads as costume.

## The Image — Honest Read

The Michelangelo/Gucci composite is a good internal piece and cannot be the brand:

1. **Built on another house's registered trademark.** Real exposure, and it says the quiet
   part out loud: *we borrow status.* A luxury house never wears someone else's monogram.
2. **Renaissance pastiche is the dominant cliché of crypto/AI marketing** — the visual
   signature of exactly the hype cycle our headline distances us from.
3. **Grandiose in the month grandiosity stopped working.** Visual equivalent of "14 years
   of AI" — an unearned claim we already decided to drop.
4. **The opposite of Tom Ford**, which is reduction and self-assurance. Borrowed iconography
   is what a brand does when it doesn't trust its own.

**Constructive alternative:** the **Ankole bull is already in our mark**, Ankole cattle are
a genuine African symbol of wealth and lineage, and Studex Meat trades in Ankole. That's our
own mythology, nobody else owns it, and it ties the group together. Michelangelo belongs to
everyone, which means it belongs to no one.

Briefed a photographic direction: Ankole horns close under single light, real African
landscape at dusk, trade-as-craft (hands, documents, signatures), one image per view at
scale, film grain. Not: Renaissance, stock global-business, robots/brains/neural graphics,
other brands' logos, collages, gradients.
