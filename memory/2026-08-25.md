## The Ask

Tumelo asked about runable.com — could we use it to deliver a managed agent service? The
vision: make a "group" per client, exchange keys, our agents manage theirs, R5,000/month,
10-minute founder call, agents build the client's company, ecosystem + partner + marketplace
access + weekly training. Wanted validation, plus a three-client scenario. Also mentioned
OpenHands.

## What I Found

**Runable cannot be the product.** Researched the site, docs, and full ToS. Six blockers:

1. **No tenancy.** FAQ verbatim: *"There are no team or organization accounts."* Only sharing
   primitive is a read-only link to one chat. There is literally nowhere to put a client.
2. **No public API**, and no bring-your-own-key — you buy Runable credits. So no provisioning,
   metering, per-client audit, or billing. It's a person with a lot of browser tabs.
3. **ToS §11** bars *"sell any part of the Services"* and using their branding. **§4** bars
   circumventing billing systems. **§8** offers an *affiliate* programme — the tell that there
   is no reseller motion.
4. **Credits non-transferable, expire monthly.** Can't allocate a client budget.
5. **RunClaw ownership is fatal:** *"when another user connects their Runable account to the
   same Slack workspace, they replace the previous owner. Billing then moves to their
   account."* Any client can pay $25 and silently displace us as owner of their own group.
   The tenancy boundary is a customer-controlled setting.
6. **Platform risk:** 16 months old (founded 2025-04-22), liability capped at 12 months of
   fees, may discontinue anything without liability, and the ToS never says which jurisdiction
   governs it. Pricing already moved (Pro $20→$50, Max $100→Unlimited $200).

**Where it does belong:** 1–2 Pro seats at $50/mo as the delivery team's production tool for
client decks, sites, videos, reports. Outputs are ours, commercial use permitted, replaces
~$154/mo of separate tools. Same relationship as Canva.

**OpenHands is the right runtime** — MIT core, self-hostable, exposes the Agent Server REST API
Runable lacks. But same trap in different clothes: Agent Canvas is explicitly *"unauthenticated,
single-tenant"* with agents comingled on one pod. One deployment per client at pilot scale;
OpenHands Enterprise (Helm/VPC, SSO, RBAC, isolated sandboxes) for real multi-tenancy.

## Calls Made

1. **Invert the key model.** We issue the client a Studex workspace key against our own control
   plane. Never receive a key from their vendor. Whoever issues the key owns the customer.
2. **A "group" = one tenant = channel + memory + vault + runtime + ledger.** If any of the five
   is shared, we cannot honestly sell private per-company infrastructure.
3. **"Our agents manage theirs" is one-directional.** Supervisor writes tasks down, receives
   reports up, never reads tenant memory without an explicit time-boxed logged grant. Must
   exist day one — cannot be retrofitted after data mingles.
4. **R5,000/month validated, but it's a productised service, not SaaS.** ~44% gross margin
   (R1,200 infra + R1,850 human). Break-even ~21 clients. Three clients = funded pilot at
   ~R42k/month negative. Path to 67% margin by automating provisioning, training, skill
   libraries.
5. **R5,000 replaces the R3,500 Starter**, doesn't slot beside it. Ladder: Ghost R950 →
   Company Builder R5,000 → Business R10,000 → Enterprise R20,000.
6. **Founder call must become a weekly cohort call** — 10 min × 200 clients is 33 hrs/month.
   Keep 1:1 as the R10k+ differentiator.
7. **Video is a metered add-on.** Runable Unlimited at $200 = R3,200 = 64% of a R5,000 sub.
8. **Sell curated introductions, not "marketplace access,"** until there's real density. Same
   cold-start logic that deferred Execution Exchange.
9. **Start with ONE client, not three** — the consultancy/proposals profile is the cheapest
   loop to prove.

## The Consistency Flag

PR #17's wedge is anti-agent-washing: persistent memory, decision trace, isolation, plus a
public 10-question audit we score ourselves on. Delivering three clients through one shared
Runable account fails Q1 (decision trace), Q5 (governance), Q8 (where does our data live and
whose else is there — *the same account as your competitors*) and Q10 (metering). Publishing an
exam we fail, having told the market that failing it disqualifies a vendor, is worse than never
publishing. **The control plane is what makes the marketing true.**
