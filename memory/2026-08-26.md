## The Best Finding

**EUDR, deadline 30 December 2026.** Every coffee shipment entering the EU must carry a due
diligence statement with geolocation data for all production plots. Covers ~95% of EU coffee
imports (large/medium operators); micro/small have until 30 June 2027. The May 2026
simplification package added soluble coffee and introduced **voluntary grouping** — letting
smallholder cooperatives file consolidated declarations. That provision exists specifically for
African smallholder coffee.

Four months out, legally mandatory, data-shaped, and the compliance mechanism is purpose-built
for the cooperative structure Tumelo wants to work with. It's the Business Ghost applied to a
cooperative. Needs no export licence and no certification accreditation.

**And the obligation falls on the EU importer, not the farmer** — so willingness to pay sits on
the buyer side. That single fact reorganises the coffee business from "middleman taking 20% of
farmer revenue" to "compliance layer keeping Rwandan coffee in the European market."

## Hard Blockers Found

**Cannot certify coffee.** Fairtrade certification is carried out solely by FLOCERT — ISO 17065
accredited since 2007, audited annually by DAkkS and Fairtrade International. Rainforest Alliance
uses authorised CBs. Critically, ISO 17065 requires the certifier to be *independent of the party
certified* — so taking a cut of coffee revenue AND certifying that coffee is the exact
arrangement the standard exists to prevent. Sell certification *readiness* instead (unregulated,
valuable).

**The 20% farmer cut is self-defeating.** Fairtrade minimum is $1.80/lb ($2.40 organic). Take 20%
of gross and the farmer's net may drop below the floor — making the coffee ineligible for the
certification that generates the premium. Plus the headline risk. Recommended: uplift share above
a documented baseline, plus buyer-side compliance subscription.

**Stablecoin unlawful in both jurisdictions.** Rwanda Law N° 023/2026, gazetted 28 May 2026, in
force: CMA lead regulator, only incorporated entities licensable, and **virtual assets may not be
used as a general means of payment without BNR authorisation** — which is exactly the described
use. Implementing regulations unpublished, so compliance can't even be determined. Unauthorised
operation is criminal. Stablecoin issuers need CMA approval, full reserve backing, licensed
custodians, public proof of reserves. Algorithmic stablecoins excluded entirely.
**But: closed-loop systems are expressly carved out of the virtual asset definition** — the same
recommendation I made for SA last week, now confirmed in Rwandan statute. Also: never pay
smallholder farmers in crypto — duty of care, not just licensing.

## Calls Made

1. **These are two companies**, not one: Studex OS (software, weeks, low capital) and Studex
   Trade (commodity, seasons, high working capital). Separate entities and P&Ls, or the commodity
   side wins every budget argument because its deadlines are physical.
2. **Four phases:** sell the OS now → EUDR pilot in parallel (deadline-driven) → Rwanda
   institutional after the pilot produces a receipt → trade layer + closed-loop credits last.
3. **Pricing = three layers.** Subscription (access + memory) + credits (work consumed) +
   marketplace (skills + extras). **New Standard tier at R2,500** — R950 → R5,000 was too wide a
   jump, and Studex Rise can't afford R5,000. 1 credit = R0.20.
4. **Marketplace split 80/20 favouring creators**, matching MuleRun. At launch creators are the
   scarce side; taxing the scarce side early doesn't get a second attempt. Raise later, grandfather
   early creators.
5. **R149 day pass, not a free day.** Free costs real inference money and attracts non-buyers;
   paying once transforms conversion. And we can't win a free-tier race against MuleRun's 200
   credits/day forever.
6. **Sell outcomes, not agent-hours.** Time-based rental is supply-side thinking. "Website by
   tomorrow, R3,500" beats "rent an agent for 4 hours." Keep passes only for the trial.
7. **Hard pricing constraint:** self-serve tooling tops out at ~R2,500–3,200/mo (Runable $200,
   MuleRun ~$160, Base44 $160). At R5,000 we lose any feature comparison — must sell managed
   outcome, never tooling.
8. **Positioning:** *"An operating system for companies that don't have one."* Then: big companies
   have memory, an execution team and market access; we rent all three by the month. Never says
   "agentic."
9. **Reframe the research loops.** Scheduling architecture right, objective wrong. Open-ended
   money discovery produces plausible unverifiable suggestions forever — and in a business whose
   positioning is "here are our receipts," a loop that can't produce one is the most dangerous
   thing we could build. Every loop needs a specific question, a verifiable sourced output, and a
   human gate. Drop 3-hour cadence (400 runs/day at 50 clients).
10. **Offline = offline data capture**, not offline inference. For EUDR plot mapping in rural
    Rwanda it's a requirement, not a feature.

## Rwanda Institutional Timing

Unusually good and narrow. National AI Agency approved by Cabinet **8 June 2026** — eleven weeks
old, still defining its programme. Mandate covers commercialisation, industry growth,
international engagement. 2023 National AI Policy names **agriculture** a priority sector.
**USD 76.5M implementation plan, only ~USD 1.2M mobilised.** Acknowledged weak data-sharing and
governance frameworks. Data Protection and Privacy Law (2021) in force with an established DPO.
Tier-3 national data centre, 98% mobile penetration, limited GPU capacity. NST2 targets digital
literacy 53% → 100% by 2029.

Approach: bring a *working* EUDR deployment, not a proposal. Enter via ICT Chamber and C4IR, not
the ministry. Design data residency in from the start — "the data stays in Rwanda" is one of the
strongest offers we have to a government that has publicly named data governance as a gap.

## NAEB Export Requirements (if we ever move physical coffee)

Licence free of charge via RDB One Stop Centre. Validity conflicting — OSC/RwandaTrade say 5
years, NAEB's exporter page says 1 year renewable; **verify with NAEB**. Needs: supplier
contract, export performance report, QA staff certificate, business registration, coffee washing
station letter, TIN, Irembo account, and a NAEB warehouse inspection. **Minimum 1 container
(19.2 tons) per season** (smaller allowed for high-value e.g. Cup of Excellence). Per shipment:
certificate of origin + quality (NAEB), phytosanitary (RALIS).
